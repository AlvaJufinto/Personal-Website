I've seen so MANY people posting their achievements on LinkedIn lately. Internship announcements, competition wins, new positions, personal projects, and certifications. It's even become a running joke in my friend group's chat. You open LinkedIn for five minutes and suddenly someone has announced that they've landed an internship. Wkwkwk.

![Whenever I open LinkedIn these days](https://media.licdn.com/dms/image/v2/C5622AQF-HHtPlkj34Q/feedshare-shrink_800/feedshare-shrink_800/0/1651811445708?e=2147483647&t=cC5LJqiCfwdpP_RcYunF3Cf8Iuf-Oki5fZRalb6yH-U&v=beta)

Don't get me wrong, I'm happy for them. They worked hard for it, and they deserve it. But seeing these announcements every day somehow makes me question whether I'm doing enough with my life. It starts to drive me crazy. And ironically, I'm participating in this rat race, this life ladder of doom and despair, too. I join competitions, build projects, learn new technologies, and pursue opportunities. Sometimes I feel like I'm just another product in that Buzz Lightyear toy meme.

> "I thought I was unique, but nope. I'm just another regular product on the shelf."

## Another product on the shelf

The funny thing is, everyone is trying to stand out, but we're all doing pretty much the same things. Building projects, joining hackathons, collecting achievements, and trying to make our CVs look more impressive. Then you open LinkedIn and realize thousands of other people are doing exactly the same thing. Someone wins an international competition, someone gets into a big tech company, and someone else is already building a startup.

Good for them, honestly. But suddenly, your own achievements don't feel that special anymore. It's like there's always someone doing something bigger, and somehow you feel obligated to catch up.

## Different paths, same race?

I've been thinking about this. We often see getting a corporate job as the conventional career path. Graduate, get hired, get promoted, increase your salary, and eventually become someone important in the company. Pretty straightforward, right?

Then there's entrepreneurship. Build your own product, get users, generate revenue, find investors, and scale the business. Sounds more exciting than spending your life working for someone else.

But are we really escaping the rat race by becoming entrepreneurs? Employees chase promotions and salary increments, while founders chase revenue, users, and business growth. Different paths, but both come with their own endless milestones. You finally get promoted, now you want a managerial position. Your startup finally gets traction, now you need to scale it.

**When does it actually stop?**

I'm not saying ambition is bad. I want to build something of my own someday, too. But sometimes I wonder whether we're pursuing these things because we genuinely want them or because we've convinced ourselves that this is what success is supposed to look like.

## I kinda miss the old days

I kinda miss when finishing a small personal project was enough to make me happy. It didn't need hundreds of users, a business model, or some fancy AI integration. Sometimes, getting a stupid feature to work after hours of debugging was already a massive accomplishment.

Now, I catch myself asking whether a project is portfolio-worthy, whether recruiters would find it interesting, or whether it's impressive enough to share on LinkedIn. Seriously, when did I start treating my personal projects like another job application?

And then came AI. We can build things much faster now, which is great. I use these tools almost every day. But somehow, the expectations keep getting higher, too. We can build faster, so we should build more. We can learn faster, so we should learn more. There's always another tool to explore and another skill to acquire.

I don't think AI created this problem, but it certainly makes keeping up feel even more exhausting. Sometimes I wonder whether we're actually becoming more productive or just finding new ways to keep running.

## How much is enough?

I don't have a profound answer to this. I'm still figuring things out myself. I still want to become a better engineer, build interesting products, and hopefully start something of my own someday. I don't want to stop growing or turn down opportunities just because I'm tired of competing.

But I also don't want to spend my entire life chasing achievements just to convince myself that I'm doing well. I don't want to build a startup just because being a founder sounds cooler than having a regular job. And I don't want to pursue another promotion just to have a better title on my LinkedIn profile.

Maybe I just need to stop treating every milestone as a starting point for the next one. It's okay to want more, but I hope I can learn to appreciate what I've already accomplished, too.

Anyway, I guess I'm still another Buzz Lightyear on the shelf. Just trying to figure out whether I actually want to keep running or whether I'd rather get off this damn shelf.
