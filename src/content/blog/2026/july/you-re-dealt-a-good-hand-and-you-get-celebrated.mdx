Have you ever noticed how some people seem to be born with every advantage? They're naturally intelligent, conventionally attractive, tall, musically gifted, financially secure, or surrounded by the right people. Then they're celebrated for succeeding, even though many of those advantages were never earned.

Meanwhile, someone else has to work twice as hard just to reach the same starting line.

I've been thinking about that a lot lately,
someone with whole ace cards in their deck.

I believe in hard work, and I try to give my best to the things that matter to me. But I'd be lying if I said luck doesn't play a role. 

Still, sometimes I catch myself feeling jealous of people who simply have better cards than I do.

Maybe they were born into wealth. Maybe they have an exceptional IQ, natural charisma, a supportive network, or talents that open doors effortlessly. Whatever the advantage is, it compounds over time, putting them in a position where success becomes exponentially easier.

Life really does feel like a lottery sometimes.

That doesn't mean I haven't been given good cards of my own. I know I have strengths that other people might wish they had. I work hard, and I try to play my hand as well as I can.

I believe the cards are shuffling though. When you lose something in life (your best card), you'll get better cards, and that will be your chance to flip the game up. Even if it is just an ace card in the pocket. 

In the end, I still wonder how far I would go if I had been dealt someone else's hand instead, or how far they would go if they had been dealt mine.

These thoughts were inspired by *The Men That Drive Me Places* by Ben Rector, an underrated song from 2015. In it, Rector reflects on the man who drives him from city to city. Both of them work hard, yet only one receives the applause, recognition, and admiration. The difference isn't necessarily effort. It's the hand life dealt them.
